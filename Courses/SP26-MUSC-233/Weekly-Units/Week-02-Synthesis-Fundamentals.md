## Overview

Building on synthesis concepts from MUSC 232, this week deepens your understanding of subtractive, FM, and wavetable synthesis. We'll focus on creating UI sounds, textures, and electronic elements that are applicable across film, games, podcasts, and other media contexts.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

- Apply subtractive synthesis techniques to create UI and interface sounds
- Use FM synthesis to generate bell-like tones and metallic textures
- Explore wavetable synthesis for evolving pads and textures
- Create cohesive sound palettes using synthesis techniques

## Topics Covered

### Review: Synthesis Building Blocks

- Oscillators and waveforms (sine, saw, square, triangle)
- Filters (low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, notch)
- Envelopes (ADSR) and their impact on sound
- LFOs and modulation routing

### Subtractive Synthesis

**Core Concepts**
- Starting with harmonically rich waveforms
- Sculpting with filters and resonance
- Envelope shaping for dynamics
- Creating movement with modulation

**Applications**
- UI clicks and button sounds
- Notification tones
- Bass textures
- Pad sounds

### FM Synthesis

**Core Concepts**
- Carriers and modulators
- Ratio relationships and harmonics
- FM index and brightness
- Envelope modulation of FM amount

**Applications**
- Bell tones and chimes
- Metallic textures
- Electric piano-style sounds
- Digital percussion

### Wavetable Synthesis

**Core Concepts**
- Wavetable scanning and position
- Morphing between wave shapes
- Frame modulation for movement
- Layering wavetables

**Applications**
- Evolving pads and textures
- Modern electronic sounds
- Sound design elements
- Sci-fi and futuristic tones

### Practical Synthesis Workflow

- Sound design from a brief (what sound do you need?)
- Building sounds from scratch vs. starting with presets
- Layering and combining synthesis methods
- Exporting and organizing synthesized assets

## In-Class Activity

**Synthesis Sound Design Workshop**

**Part 1: UI Sound Set (30 min)**

Using subtractive synthesis (Vital or DAW synth), create:

1. **Button Click** - Short, satisfying click
   - Fast attack, short decay
   - High-pass filtered
   - Subtle pitch envelope

2. **Confirmation Tone** - Positive feedback sound
   - Two-note rising phrase
   - Clean sine or triangle base
   - Gentle reverb

3. **Error/Alert** - Attention-getting without being harsh
   - Slightly dissonant interval
   - Moderate decay
   - Consider adding subtle tremolo

4. **Notification Ping** - Clear, pleasant alert
   - Bell-like quality (try FM)
   - Medium sustain
   - Soft attack

**Part 2: Texture Creation (25 min)**

Using wavetable or FM synthesis, create:

1. **Evolving Pad** - Slow-moving atmospheric texture
   - Wavetable position modulated by LFO
   - Long attack and release
   - Add subtle filter movement

2. **Metallic Texture** - Industrial or mechanical feel
   - FM synthesis with low ratio
   - Ring modulation or detuning
   - Experiment with resonance

**Part 3: Sound Palette Integration (25 min)**

1. Create a cohesive set of 6 sounds that could work together in a project
2. Consider consistency in:
   - Frequency range
   - Reverb/space characteristics
   - Tonal quality
3. Export all sounds (48kHz/24-bit WAV)
4. Use naming convention: `Synth_Category_Description_V1.wav`

**Deliverable:**
Folder containing your 6-sound palette, properly named and organized.
